Replica

A site specific temporary installation which explores portraits, digital replications, and imagery.

<This project was co-produced with three of my teammates.>
A 15 minute experience for a 100 people crowd, involving DMX Lighting, Projectors, Live Cameras, Live Audio, and a lot of screens -both digital and fabric-. Our goal was to have the audience walk through the space in three levels, experiencing the absurdity of media replications of themselves.

A one-week project in response to a guest lecture from the class [Applications] at MPS ITP, New York University. As a team of 4, we had to create a group experience for a 100 people, in less then a week's time.

My team decided to play with media, screens, replicas and images, using cameras to capture the people in the room and live-project them in the same space. We ended up involving DMX Lighting in the Media Commons production spaces for cuing and dramatic effect. Below are the videos of the team putting together the experience.

DMX after Project Replica

Introduced to DMX Lighting in the workplace as a Media Commons Production Assistant, and after experiencing what DMX Lighting offers through the Replica project -which I considered a big success, especially considering that this massive project was less then a week in production with only 4 people on the team- I got very interested in DMX Lighting. More so because not a lot of people were using it in the Production spaces in 370J, nor knew about them.

So I proposed & worked on a project that would make this process easier for everyone. Below is a video demonstration of what my custom scene brought to the space. QLC+, which is the open source software we used in Media Commons back then, did not have template files in them, so I created some options for people to play around with.

This one, I named 'Pasodoble'

Now, this QLC+ scene is frozen on the production computers for people to access much easily. And along with it, I've written DMX guides for people to learn how to use the softwares.
